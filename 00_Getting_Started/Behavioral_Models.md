# Behavioral Models

Behavioral models are useful when the goal is to study a system relationship before committing to a transistor-level implementation. They must be labelled clearly and separated from physical device models. A behavioral source can hide limiting, delay, noise, discontinuity, or convergence assumptions.

When introducing a behavioral model, document its equation, valid operating range, parameters, expected units, and limitations. For a nonlinear resistor, compare behavioral, polynomial, subcircuit, and physical-device implementations in the dedicated modelling section rather than duplicating the same experiment under multiple analysis names.
